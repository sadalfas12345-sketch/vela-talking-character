# Tripo reference character and facial controls

The original image-to-3D output and the Tripo Mixamo body rig remain intact. No additional Tripo generation or rigging call was made for this conversion.

| Asset | Purpose | Facial state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `vela-reference-original.glb` | Original Tripo character, 261,084 vertices, 487,706 triangles; 8K base-color and 4K normal/ORM textures | No bones or morph targets |
| `vela-reference-rigged.glb` | Original Tripo Mixamo output, 65 body bones | No facial bones or morph targets |
| `vela-reference-faced.glb` | Derived TalkingHead-ready output, same 261,084 vertex mesh, original body rig, materials and textures | 29 real mesh deformation targets |
| `face-transfer-calibration.json` | Measured MediaPipe landmarks in the original mesh coordinate frame | Input to the reproducible Blender conversion |
| `facial-rig-report.json` | Conversion report and target list | Generated by the Blender script |

## Geometry inspection

The generated face is part of the main textured body mesh. The lips meet at a closed seam; there is no modeled mouth cavity, separate upper/lower dental mesh, tongue, or facial bone set. Eye and lash surfaces are not independent eye objects in the Tripo GLB. The mesh is dense enough to move the exterior skin surface locally, but it cannot reveal a realistic mouth interior without new geometry and retopology. The conversion intentionally preserves the existing appearance and does not invent teeth.

The original and Mixamo outputs retain the same vertex count, but Mixamo alters vertex coordinates and triangulated face ordering, so direct donor vertex transfer is unsafe. The conversion registers landmarks into the rigged mesh using the measured bounding-box vertical offset. The Vela Avaturn donor has 71 facial targets including visemes, blinks, smiles, frowns, and brow shapes, but its facial proportions and topology differ. We inspected aligned frontal renders and MediaPipe landmarks. Direct one-to-one vertex transfer is not valid, and nonrigid donor fitting would produce identity-distorting patches on the Tripo face, especially around the closed mouth and fringe. The implemented route instead uses the Tripo's own measured facial landmarks to create localized geometry deltas on its surface. The original face texture, hair, mesh, weights, and Mixamo bones are retained.

## What animates

The derived mesh has actual position deltas for `jawOpen`, `mouthOpen`, all 15 Oculus `viseme_*` targets, independent `eyeBlinkLeft` and `eyeBlinkRight`, `mouthSmileLeft/Right`, `mouthFrownLeft/Right`, and brow up/down targets. They are exported as glTF morph targets and are applied by TalkingHead's existing animation and lip-sync loop. The mouth/lip seam moves with the synthesized visemes, but the closed shell visibly stretches instead of opening into a cavity. Treat the speech as surface lip-sync rather than a complete oral rig. Blink, smile/frown, and brow motion deform the face surface; there are no separate eye balls for gaze rotation.

The Mixamo rig omits eye anchor bones that TalkingHead expects for its camera and speech animation paths. Vela adds two eye anchors at the measured target landmarks at import time. It also supplies no-op internal channels for optional eye-gaze controls absent from this mesh and filters missing randomized targets, preventing the animation loop from throwing. It does not claim that those internal channels create eye-gaze geometry.

## Reproduce and verify

From the repository root on Windows with Blender 5.x:

```powershell
& 'C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.2\blender.exe' -b --python scripts\build_tripo_face_rig.py
npm.cmd run test:tripo-face
```

The Blender script reads `vela-reference-rigged.glb`, verifies its 261,084-vertex mesh and body weights, measures the vertical offset against the original mesh, and writes `vela-reference-faced.glb` plus a JSON report. It never edits either source GLB. The Playwright test uploads the derivative into Vela, checks actual projected vertex deformation and rendered frames for jaw, both blinks, smile, frown, and brow, synthesizes speech through local HeadTTS, captures the live WebGL canvas during an open-vowel viseme, samples mouth/blink/expression channels, and rejects activation failures or browser exceptions. Captured frames are in `verification/`.

## Other methods assessed

- **Vela Avaturn deformation transfer:** the donor has the required shapes but differs in identity and facial proportions. Landmark overlays showed adequate feature correspondence for registering controls, not enough to copy its full vertex deltas safely. Transferring its head topology would also replace the Tripo identity. Not used.
- **CaptureForge / FaceForge:** GPL-3.0-or-later Blender extension. The current upstream `captureforge/face/autofit.py` is runnable and fits a 26-bone jaw/eye/lid/brow/lip/tongue rig from MediaPipe landmarks, then computes distance-falloff skin weights. The Blender add-on is pure Python with no extra in-Blender packages and supports Blender 4.4+; the fitting helper needs a separate configured Python with MediaPipe and its Face Landmarker model. It creates the rig, not the finished ARKit performance: its documented shape-key workflow still requires manually posing each marker and baking evaluated geometry. Its separate-object eye/teeth/tongue support is useful, but this Tripo face has no separate eyes or mouth parts. The repo specifies no CUDA or minimum GPU requirement; it was not used because the measured surface controls were faster to make and directly exportable to Vela.
- **Tripo Face Rig (`mdj128/facial-animation`):** MIT-licensed and runnable with Blender 5.1.1 or newer, with Rhubarb Lip Sync 1.14 for its sample audio workflow. It is artist-assisted and expects a prepared head plus separate upper/lower teeth and tongue objects for a convincing speaking mouth. The Tripo output has no separate mouth parts; its mouth detector reported no mouth profile. This route could create more complete mouth geometry only after a manual mouth-separation/retopology step that would alter the current mesh, so it was not silently applied.
- **OmniFaceRig:** the authors report 20–30 seconds per asset on one A100 GPU and up to 155 blendshapes plus fitted teeth, gums, and tongue. As of this check, the official `facebookresearch/omnifacerig` repository contains the released rigged dataset, scripts, and tests, but no model inference/rigging pipeline or weights to run on the Tripo GLB. The research materials use Meta FAIR's noncommercial research license. It is the most promising future conversion route, but cannot currently be benchmarked on this RTX 2060 6 GB PC or applied without the missing inference pipeline.

No method can create an anatomically correct mouth cavity from the present closed surface without adding/interior topology. The derived GLB provides the maximum tested subset that preserves the current character and uses Vela's existing runtime.
