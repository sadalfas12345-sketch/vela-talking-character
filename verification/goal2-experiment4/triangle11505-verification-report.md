# Experiment 4 triangle 11505 verification

Baseline: commit `1eb03d21bf6b43c99b3628a6ea970402e5d8ca41`

The ARAP solver, landmark 70 target, one-ring support, and source mesh were not changed.

## Correct validation rule

The previous inversion counter compared each deformed triangle normal with its own neutral normal. That is not a valid fold test by itself: a connected surface can rotate more than 90 degrees while remaining locally continuous.

This audit uses three local tests over 21 samples from neutral to the existing full landmark target:

1. Every triangle area remains finite and positive.
2. The signed side of each edge-sharing opposite vertex remains on its original side of the common edge.
3. The edge-sharing dihedral remains defined and is inspected for a local fold.

The second test detects the actual event that matters here: the triangle crossing through its shared edge into the neighboring face.

Normal rotation relative to the neutral triangle is 107.621 degrees at the full target. That value alone would be inconclusive.

The edge-sharing dihedral changes smoothly from 1.454 degrees at neutral to 61.737 degrees at the full target. However, the signed-side test finds the decisive local event:

- At 65%: triangle 11505 and neighbor 11501 are still on opposite sides of their shared edge.
- At approximately 68.9846%: the opposite vertex of triangle 11505 crosses the shared-edge plane.
- At 70% and later: both opposite vertices are on the same side of the shared edge.

Therefore the triangle has crossed through its neighboring face. It is a genuine local fold, not a false positive caused only by normal rotation. The positive triangle area does not rescue it: a triangle can remain nondegenerate while folding through an adjacent face.
